Interlude — Inland

The forest keeps its own tide.

A quarter mile behind the cove, the salt air softens into cedar shade. Here water arrives not in waves, but in mist and patient rain, gathered stitch by stitch along the bark.

Moss is the tide of the woods—swelling lush through winter damp, drawing pale and quiet in the height of August, keeping its own slow calendar between the seasons.
